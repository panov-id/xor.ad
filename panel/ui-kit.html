A component names a role and never a colour, so the dark theme is a second set of values rather than a second set of rules. The values below are read from the running page, so this table cannot fall behind the stylesheet either.

Relief

Two roles, told apart before they are read. A field is a hole you put something into: sunk, and sunk whether or not it is focused, because what it is does not change when you look at it. A button is a thing sitting on the page: it lifts to meet the pointer and is pushed in when clicked, and the shadow travels with it rather than merely darkening.

The three volumes

--depth-rest at rest, --depth-hover under the pointer, --sunk pressed — and --sunk again for anything you type into, which is the whole idea: pressed and "a hole" are the same shape.

Buttons

No class needed. A <button> anywhere in the panel is already in the language — that is the point of the shared rule, and the reason four pages written after the old selector list were wearing browser defaults until it existed.

States

Hover and press are not screenshots — put a pointer on them. Disabled does not stand up: it loses its shadow as well as its ink.

In a table row

.row-action and .state-action are spacing, not looks: the relief already came from being a button.

Segmented range

The chosen range is filled and held down — aria-pressed is a click that stayed, so the state is in the markup a screen reader reads, not only in a class.

Fields

Also no class. Sunk, framed, and the same size as the text around them.

Ticks

A native checkbox cannot carry relief — the browser draws it and ignores a shadow — so it is replaced rather than decorated. Standing up while empty, pushed in and filled once chosen. The square corner is the only thing telling it apart from a radio, which is why the radius is half the shared one.

Welded forms

.auth-form is the one place the vocabulary is deliberately broken: the fields and the button share a single box, so the button cannot travel — it would tear a hole in the border it shares — and presses in place instead. On a narrow screen the box welds top to bottom rather than crushing three things onto one line.
