## D3 — the node accepted; delivery after offline; no delivered or read signal

**Decision (refined 2026-09-12).** The node keeps the ciphertext until delivery or
until the conversation dies, whichever comes first, and hands it to the other person
when they come back. To the sender the node reports exactly one thing: **accepted**.
Neither "delivered" nor "read" exists as a state on the sender's side — otherwise the
status becomes a presence oracle again. `error` means only "the node did not answer
or refused". The ciphertext is unreadable to the node, and that is the point: holding
someone's encrypted bytes until delivery does not contradict the product; signalling
someone's presence does.

**Consequence for the order.** This is not a text edit: the decision overturns the
decision of 2026-08-10, the title of §8.8, two acceptance criteria and promises in the
published legal texts of both storefronts. Everything is named below.

**Now.**
- `chat_RU.md:695` — "if the recipient is on no node, the message simply goes
  nowhere and the sender gets `error`";
- `chat_RU.md:2036-2037` — `delivered — the other participant accepted`,
  `error — …offline…`;
- `08-chat-screen_RU.md:53` — "'✓' — the node passed it on";
- the mechanics already say the right thing: `00-mechanics_RU.md:702` ("if the
  other person is offline — until delivery or the end of the conversation").

**Edit — the canon.**
- `chat_RU/EN.md:695`: "…if the recipient is absent, the node puts the ciphertext
  into `pending_deliveries` and hands it over on connect; the row lives until delivery
  or until the conversation dies. The sender is not told: the node's answer is
  'accepted' and nothing else".
- The §8.8 title `:2018` "Messages: they are not in the DB" → "Messages: the node
  keeps only the undelivered"; `:2020` ("There is no `messages` table… history only in
  browsers") rewritten: there is no history, there is a queue of the undelivered;
  `:684` ("messages are not written to the DB") and `:2231` likewise.
- **The decision of 2026-08-10 `:2220-2229` ("There will be no auto-retry when the
  other person comes back") is marked `[retired]`** with a reference to this decision.
  Its argument — "any delivery 'later' requires the message to sit somewhere" — holds;
  what changed is that we accept that, because the bytes are unreadable.
- `:2036-2037`: two states remain — `accepted — the node accepted`, `error — the node
  did not answer or refused`. The word `delivered` leaves the canon entirely
  (`chat-flows_*:559`, `depth-client_*:535/553` too).
- `:2040` ("`local_id`… is stored nowhere") rewritten: `local_id` sits in the queue and
  is the key against duplicates.
- `:2200-2204` (the missed-message indicator, "the text will never come back") — up for
  revision: after D3 it does come back if the peer returns while the conversation lives.
- The sliding conversation deadline counts from the node's acceptance, not from the
  peer's (`chat_EN.md:53`, `:1978`, `:2000` and the RU pairs).

**Edit — the schema.** The table appears at **step 6** of the build order, together
with §8.13: at step 5 the messages are still in the clear (`chat_RU.md:2506-2508`) and
must not be put into the database. It is not in the ten-table first migration.

```sql
CREATE TABLE pending_deliveries (
  chat              uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES chats(id)    ON DELETE CASCADE,
  recipient_session uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES sessions(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  local_id          uuid NOT NULL,
  ciphertext        bytea NOT NULL,
  created_at        timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  PRIMARY KEY (chat, recipient_session, local_id)
);
CREATE INDEX ON pending_deliveries (recipient_session, created_at);
```

- Every insert is `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`; a sender's retry with the same `local_id`
  produces no duplicate.
- **Rows are deleted** on the recipient's acknowledgement, and in the same transactions
  that burn the share on a move or a freeze (`:1007`), set `gone_at` for either of the
  two (the key `K` dies on the first death, `:2318`) and close an identity. "Cascade
  from `chats`" is not enough on its own: the `chats` row lives until `gone_at` stands
  for both (`:2111`).
- A **cap** `chat.pending.max` per (conversation, recipient) pair and `chat.pending.ttl`
  in `docs/facts/limits.tsv`, enforced by the node. Overflow evicts the oldest silently:
  a refusal on overflow would again signal "they have been away for a long time".
- The row `pending_deliveries docs/chat_RU.md:<line> - product` goes into
  `docs/facts/schema.tsv`; the count of unmigrated tables in `open.tsv` 24 → 25.
- Extend `check-identity-cascades.sh`: a reference to `sessions` must name `ON DELETE`
  too, or the table slips past the gate.

**Edit — delivery.**
- One order for both cases: `INSERT` → answer "accepted" → hand over → delete on the
  **recipient's** acknowledgement. There must be no difference in latency between
  "online" and "offline": that is the same oracle, told by the clock.
- The recipient's acknowledgement is added to the protocol (`protocol_RU/EN.md`).
- Extend the 2026-09-11 decision "v1 fans out from process memory" (`:706`): memory
  serves the live, the database holds the undelivered; on connect the node registers
  the socket first and only then reads the queue.

**Edit — operations.**
- The table is excluded from the nightly dump:
  `pg_dump --exclude-table-data=pending_deliveries` in
  `relay/wizard/backup-postgres.sh` (otherwise the ciphertext lives in the copies for
  another 14 days, `:43`). The restore drill does not break: transit is not restored.
- Three metrics in `/metrics`: rows, bytes, age of the oldest row. An age above the
  longest conversation deadline means the janitor has stopped.
- The conversation janitor (`gone_at`, deleting `chats`) does not exist in the code:
  `relay/node/src/lib/scheduled.ts` has no task about conversations at all. Before D3
  that went unnoticed; after it the queue is never cleaned. An item in `open.tsv`.

**Edit — criteria and external texts.**
- `chat_RU/EN.md:2587-2590` and `docs/test-map_RU/EN.md` 12.1 and 12.6: "there is not
  a single message in the database" → "there is no plaintext; `pending_deliveries` is

- `docs/dpia_RU/EN.md:68`, `:72`; `docs/article-30-register_RU/EN.md:98` — a new

- The published texts of both storefronts: `landing/legal/privacy_EN.md:40`, `:77`,
  `landing/legal/terms_EN.md:69`, `landing/legal/community-guidelines_*` — "not stored

**Cost.** The ciphertext sits on the server while the recipient is offline. The node

## D6 — PIN before every irreversible action

**Decision.** Moving the identity, changing the PIN, "erase conversation history"
and "start over" are carried out by the node only with a fresh proof of the PIN —
the same one used when handing out the share (`chat_RU.md:1143`), with the same
counter of ten. Changing the PIN requires the **old** PIN.

**Now.** `POST /sessions/invite` needs only a signature (`chat_RU.md:999`,
`protocol_RU.md:75`); screen 12 (`:17`, `:51-53`, `:59`, `:74`) names no repeat PIN.

**Edit.**

  decided 2026-09-11": the four actions, the `auth` proof from `vault_shares`, the

## Addenda after the panel — 2026-09-12

The panel on this plan (`PANEL_2026-09-11_plan-D1-D6.md`) found missed places and two
holes in the wording itself. Only what was not above is here.

**D6 — the path after a burned share.** The canon says only that the identity is
intact (`chat_RU.md:1162`). In the web the signing key sits outside the share
(`:1186`, `:2322`), so a device with a burned share goes on signing requests. Without
a rule it is one of two things: either "no share, nothing to check", and the PIN is
bypassed by ten deliberate wrong entries, or "refuse", and an owner who forgot the PIN
is locked out for good. We write: the share is burned — all four actions refuse, and
the only way out is the paper code; recovery mints a new share and a new PIN and
returns the counter to ten. In `depth` the question does not arise: the key file there
is encrypted with the same vault key (`:1253`).

**D6 — "erase conversation history" leaves the list.** History lives only on the
device (§8.8), the node has nothing to execute, and spending the shared counter's
attempts on a client-side check is theatre. A client-side check remains. The list of
node-protected actions holds three: move, PIN change, start over.

**D6 — burning the share must be one thing.** The canon has three today: `DELETE FROM
vault_shares` (`:1007`), `share_enc bytea NOT NULL` (`:1149`) and "burning writes
`share_enc = NULL`" (`:1158`) — the last would fail on `NOT NULL`. Bring them to one:
`share_enc bytea`, `CHECK ((burned_at IS NULL) = (share_enc IS NOT NULL))`, burning is
`UPDATE … SET share_enc = NULL, burned_at = now()`. The counter decrement goes in its
own short transaction, so that rolling back a refused action does not undo it.

**D6 — the handles do not exist.** `protocol_RU/EN.md:71-107` has no routes for "start
over" and "change the PIN", so the plan's "neighbouring handles" are imaginary. We name
them as new, marked "proposed", together with the PIN-proof field. The word "fresh"
goes: it is undefined; the proof is derived from the typed PIN afresh for every action
and sits inside the signed body.

**D5 — "we do not attach who you are" is not said for a chat.** A chat report has
target `chat`, and a chat is known to two people, so the notifier is deduced by the
other one (`dsa/SPEC_RU.md:424`, `08-chat-screen_RU.md:19`). The support route is
linked to the identity as well (`14-support_RU.md:30`). On screen 5 the line stays for
the feed; on screens 8 and 14 we write the truth: the other person will work out that
it was you.

**D5 — §6 of the spec is edited, not §7.** The answer to the notifier is
`dsa/SPEC_RU.md:382-391`, and `:289` ("an answer is always mandatory") is there too. §7
is about the statement of reasons to the author. The argument for `open.tsv` is written
out in full: 16(4) is conditional in its text, 16(5) only through the link.

**D2 — missed places.** `chat_RU.md:1829` (the canon's disclaimer, "attaching a copy"),
`:2334`, the range `:2132-2151` instead of `2147`, `chat-flows_*`,
`depth-client_RU.md:272-273`, `test-map` 16.6, spec §3 `:103-106`, the community
guidelines of both storefronts ("your device attaches the copy", 17 language versions in
`sosed.place`, 10 in `neighbro.place`) and `privacy_EN.md:59`.

**D4 — missed places.** `chat-flows_RU.md:680-683` ("the board… is opaque to the node" —
stale since 2026-09-09), the canon's disclaimer `:1821-1829` without the "the game is
the exception" line. EN pairs: `chat_EN.md:145`, `:2075`; `18-game-in-chat_EN.md:78` has
a translation error — "A word guessed" instead of "a word set".

**D1 — the purpose of the thirty days.** The "return window" argument is gone and the
plan named no other, while storage without a purpose sits badly with storage
limitation. Either we name the purpose (handling notices tied to an identity) or we
delete at once. Together with closing an identity, in one transaction:
`recovery_auth_hash = NULL`, `recovery_wrapped_key = NULL`, sessions frozen, the share
burned, the queue cleared. An index `identities (closed_at) WHERE closed_at IS NOT NULL`
for the janitor.

**EN line numbers differ from RU.** `809→817`, `2132→2152`, `2036→2056`, `2055→2075`,
`706→709`; `147` has no pair. In the retired-terms registry every row must name its
scope; the storefront paths go through the symlinks `xor.ad/sosed.place` and
`xor.ad/neighbro.place`. The block `chat_RU.md:2137-2142` cannot be covered by one
`[retired]` marker — it has to be deleted.
